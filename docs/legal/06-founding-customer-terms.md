# 6. Founding customer terms

> **DRAFT — NOT LEGAL ADVICE. NOT ATTORNEY REVIEWED.**
> `[COUNSEL]` marks a decision a lawyer must make. `[BUILD]` marks a sentence that
> is not yet true in the product.

## What founding pricing is

Launch pricing, set lower on purpose while the product proves itself with real
businesses. The customer is taking a risk on a company without a track record, and
the price reflects that.

## What it is not

It is not a discount off a higher price, because no higher price has ever been
charged. It is not a promotion, a sale, or a limited-time offer with a deadline. It
is not a lower tier of service.

The distinction matters legally as well as commercially. Advertising a discount from
a reference price that was never charged is a recognised deceptive pricing practice.
Because founding pricing is presented as the price rather than as a saving, that
exposure does not arise, and the site must not drift into discount framing later.

`[COUNSEL]` Confirm the position holds when founding pricing ends. Announcing "the
price is going up" is fine. Displaying the old price struck through next to the new
one, or describing the founding period retroactively as a discount, is the thing to
avoid.

## Scarcity

There is none, and none may be manufactured.

No countdown, no seat count, no expiry date, no "only N remaining". The site says
this expressly and the commitment is now public.

`[COUNSEL]` If a genuine limit is ever introduced, such as a real capacity cap on
concurrent builds, it may be stated only if it is true, currently enforced, and
accurately counted. A number on a page that does not correspond to a real constraint
is a deceptive practice, and a countdown that resets is the textbook example.

## Duration

Founding pricing is available during the early launch period. No end date is
specified because none has been decided.

Required commitments:

- Business Builder will say plainly when founding pricing ends rather than
  manufacture a deadline
- A price change does not apply retroactively to a completed one-time engagement
- A price change to a recurring plan requires advance notice
  `[COUNSEL]` set the notice period, 30 days is conventional
- A founding customer's recurring rate `[COUNSEL]` decide: held indefinitely, held
  for a defined period, or subject to change on notice

That last decision has not been made and must be before anyone pays. It is the
question every founding customer will ask, and there is no defensible answer to "we
have not decided" once money has changed hands. The strongest commercial position,
holding the founding rate for as long as the subscription runs uninterrupted, is
also the easiest to state and the hardest to get wrong.

## Product maturity disclosure

The honest version. This is the section that most often goes wrong in one of two
directions: either it hides the product's stage, or it uses maturity as a blanket
excuse for everything.

The failure mode to avoid is the second: language amounting to "this is early, so
anything can break and we owe you nothing." That is not acceptable, it is unlikely
to be enforceable against a consumer under Texas law, and it contradicts the
proposition.

### Draft language

> **What being early actually means**
>
> Business Builder is new. We would rather tell you what that means than let you
> find out.
>
> **Some work is done by hand.** Parts of the build that will later run
> automatically are currently performed or closely supervised by a person. That
> generally makes the result better, not worse. It also means some steps take longer
> than they eventually will.
>
> **The product will change.** Features will be added, and how things work will
> improve. We will not remove something you are relying on without telling you
> first. `[COUNSEL]` this is a commitment, confirm it can be met
>
> **You still get what you bought.** Being early changes how the work gets done. It
> does not change the scope you agreed to, the quality expected of it, or our
> obligations to you. If we cannot deliver the agreed scope, that is our problem to
> fix or refund, not your risk to absorb.
>
> **Your privacy and security expectations do not change.** Taking founding pricing
> or joining a pilot does not lower the standard of care we owe your data or your
> customers' data.
>
> **We will ask for your feedback.** You are under no obligation to give it, and
> nothing about your pricing or service depends on whether you do.

`[COUNSEL]` Review the fourth paragraph. It deliberately concedes that failure to
deliver agreed scope is Business Builder's risk. That is the correct commercial
position and it aligns with the refund policy, but it should be a conscious choice
rather than an accident of drafting.

`[COUNSEL]` The commitment not to remove a feature a customer relies on without
notice is broad. Consider bounding it to material features and a notice period.

## Feedback and publicity

Two things to separate, because customers conflate them.

**Feedback** may be given and Business Builder may use it to improve the product,
without obligation or compensation. Standard and uncontroversial.

**Publicity** is different. Using a founding customer's name, logo, or story as a
reference requires their separate written permission, given after the fact, and
revocable.

`[COUNSEL]` Confirm publicity consent is opt-in and separate from the main terms.
Burying a publicity licence in the terms of service and then using a customer's name
is a reliable way to lose them, and in some framings it raises right-of-publicity
issues.

This matters more than usual here: the site currently carries no testimonials,
logos, or case studies, deliberately. The first real customer is the first
opportunity to have one, and the temptation to use it without proper consent will be
strongest exactly then.

## Relationship to the pilot

The first residential-cleaning customer is both a founding customer and a pilot
participant. Founding customer terms and the pilot agreement both apply. The pilot
agreement adds obligations, it does not subtract them.

`[COUNSEL]` Confirm the order of precedence where the two conflict. Recommendation:
the pilot agreement controls for pilot-specific matters, founding terms control
otherwise, and neither reduces the baseline obligations in the master terms. See
[07-pilot-service-agreement.md](07-pilot-service-agreement.md).

## Site copy alignment

Current site copy that these terms must match:

- Founding pricing is available during the early launch period
- No countdown, no seat count, no expiry date
- When founding pricing ends we will say so plainly rather than manufacture a
  deadline

All three are commitments. The terms must not contradict them, and the site must not
drift from them.
